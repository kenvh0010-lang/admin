Gall's Law: Why Over-Engineered Productivity Systems Always Fail

In 1975, systems theorist John Gall explained why complex systems designed from scratch always collapse, and why starting with a stupidly simple Version 1.0 is the only path to lasting daily output.

Why 20-Tab Dashboards Die by Thursday

If you spent all weekend building a twenty-tab productivity dashboard and stopped using it by Thursday, a nineteen seventy-five engineering rule explains why.

  • Color-coding templates feels like productive progress
  • Complex friction kills execution the first busy week

John Gall's 1975 Law of Working Systems

Systems theorist John Gall put it in his 1975 book Systemantics: a complex system that works has always evolved from a simple system that worked. A complex system designed from scratch never works.

  • Every working complex system grew out of a simple one
  • Systems designed complicated on Day 1 never survive

Designing From Scratch vs Evolving Through Reps

Beginners break this rule constantly. They design twelve-step morning routines and complicated automation pipelines before they have even shipped a single piece of finished work.

Build for Your Most Exhausted Tuesday

Flip the sequence: start with one plain checklist, one daily focus block, and one repeatable workflow you can execute even on your most exhausting Tuesday.

Earn Complexity After 30 Days of Consistency

Earn the right to add complexity only after thirty days of simple wins, otherwise.

  • Consistency beats clever system every week
  • Add automation only when manual reps work smoothly
