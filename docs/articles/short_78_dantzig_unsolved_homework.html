The Late Berkeley Grad Student Who Solved Two 'Impossible' Theorems

In 1939, UC Berkeley doctoral student George Dantzig arrived late to Professor Jerzy Neyman's statistics class, copied two famous unsolved open theorems off the chalkboard assuming they were homework, and solved both within weeks.

He Arrived Late and Solved Two Unsolved Theorems

In 1939, UC Berkeley graduate student George Dantzig walked late into Professor Jerzy Neyman's statistics class, copied two famous unsolved theorems off the chalkboard thinking they were homework, and solved both.

  • Walked late into Prof. Jerzy Neyman's 1939 lecture
  • Copied two open problems off the board as routine homework

Source: George B. Dantzig interview, College Mathematics Journal 17(4) (1986)

'A Little Harder Than Usual' Became His PhD Thesis

Days later, Dantzig left the proofs on Neyman's desk and apologized because they seemed slightly harder than usual. Six weeks later, an excited Neyman announced those solutions formed Dantzig's doctoral thesis.

  • Apologized to Neyman for taking a few extra days to finish
  • Six weeks later Neyman submitted one for publication & thesis

Source: George B. Dantzig interview, College Mathematics Journal 17(4) (1986)

Consensus Labels Shut Down Persistence Early

Dantzig later explained that had he arrived on time and heard Neyman introduce those equations as famous unsolved problems, discouragement would have made him quit after the first frustrating afternoon.

Source: George B. Dantzig interview, College Mathematics Journal 17(4) (1986)

Reframe Scary Bottlenecks as Routine Assignments

Strip intimidating prestige labels off your hardest engineering or distribution bottleneck, block ninety quiet morning minutes with pen and paper, and work the problem assuming a clean, practical solution is waiting.

Source: Mind & Margin protocol (advice, not a study)

Stop Mistaking Industry Rumors for Physical Laws

Most capable builders quit before writing line one simply because they treat borrowed industry rumors of impossibility as verified physical laws.

  • Test first principles on paper before reading forum cynicism
  • Treat hard problems like standard Tuesday coursework
