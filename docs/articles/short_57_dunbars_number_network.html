Why 5 Trusted Peers Beat 10,000 Shallow Contacts

Oxford anthropologist Robin Dunbar proved in 1992 that human brain architecture limits stable relationships to ~150 people, with an inner core of just 5. Combined with Mark Granovetter's 1973 Weak Ties research, here is a smarter way to build career relationships.

Why 10,000 Followers Still Feels Lonely

Collecting ten thousand online contacts feels productive, yet primate brain biology caps how many real relationships any human can actually sustain.

  • Digital contact lists scale infinitely on a screen
  • Human neocortex bandwidth has not changed in millennia

Source: Robin Dunbar, Journal of Human Evolution (1992)

Robin Dunbar's 1992 Neocortex Limit

In 1992, Oxford anthropologist Robin Dunbar showed in the Journal of Human Evolution that neocortex size limits stable human networks to roughly one hundred fifty people, anchored by an inner circle of five.

  • ~150 total stable social ties; ~15 close allies
  • Just 5 intimate high-trust confidants form your core

Source: Robin I. M. Dunbar, J. Human Evolution, Vol. 22, No. 6 (1992)

Shallow Feeds Starve Both Core & Weak Ties

Spreading attention thinly across noisy feeds starves both your five high-trust peers and what sociologist Mark Granovetter in 1973 called valuable weak ties.

Source: Mark S. Granovetter, 'The Strength of Weak Ties', American Journal of Sociology (1973)

Install the Weekly 5 + 2 Relationship Cadence

Build a two-tier network: invest eighty percent of your relational energy into five candid builders, and send two thoughtful no-ask notes weekly to respected weak ties.

Depth Protects You, Bridges Expand You

Depth in your inner five builds resilience, while genuine curiosity across weak ties brings fresh opportunity.

  • Your inner 5 sharpen your thinking and tell hard truths
  • Respected weak ties open doors outside your daily bubble

Source: Mind & Margin synthesis of Dunbar (1992) & Granovetter (1973)
