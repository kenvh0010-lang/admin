Why Everyday Investors Sell Winners Early and Hold Losers

Coined by Hersh Shefrin and Meir Statman in 1985 and tested across 10,000 brokerage accounts by Terrance Odean in 1998, the Disposition Effect shows how selling winners early and holding losers costs everyday investors 3.4% in subsequent annual returns.

Why Amateur Traders Cash Winners and Hug Losers

Why do everyday investors cash out winning stocks for tiny gains while clinging to sinking losers for years? Pride and regret.

  • 15% gain triggers an urge to lock in pride
  • 30% drop triggers 'hold until break-even' denial

Source: Shefrin & Statman, Journal of Finance (1985)

Shefrin, Statman & Odean's Brokerage Proof

Hersh Shefrin and Meir Statman named this the Disposition Effect in 1985. In 1998, Terrance Odean analyzed ten thousand brokerage accounts and found the sold winners went on to beat held losers by three-point-four percent.

  • Shefrin & Statman (1985) named the Disposition Effect
  • Odean (1998) verified it across 10,000 real accounts

Source: Shefrin & Statman, J. Finance (1985); Terrance Odean, J. Finance (1998)

Cutting Your Flowers to Water Your Weeds

Selling a winner locks in quick pride, while holding a loser delays admitting a mistake, leaving you trimming flowers to water your weeds.

Automate Broad Indexes or Run the Fresh-Cash Test

Erase ego from your portfolio: automate low-cost broad index funds, or ask whether you would buy a losing stock today with fresh cash.

The Market Does Not Know Your Entry Price

Judge every holding by tomorrow's math instead of yesterday's purchase price, and let true compounders run.

  • Break-even is a psychological mirage, not a strategy
  • Let automated broad indexes ride multi-decade winners
