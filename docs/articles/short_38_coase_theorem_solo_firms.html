How a 1937 Nobel Paper Predicted Tiny Million-Dollar Firms

In 1937, future Nobel laureate Ronald Coase published 'The Nature of the Firm' in Economica, showing that large companies exist only when market transaction costs are high. As software and AI shrink coordination costs, tiny high-margin firms gain a structural edge.

The 1937 Paper Behind Tiny High-Profit Firms

In 1937, a twenty-six-year-old British economist wrote a paper that quietly explains why tiny one-to-three-person software businesses now rival hundred-person agencies in profit.

  • Big revenue once required giant internal headcount
  • One economic variable changed the math

Source: Ronald Coase, 'The Nature of the Firm', Economica (1937)

Ronald Coase's Transaction Cost Discovery

In Economica, future Nobel laureate Ronald Coase asked why large corporations exist at all. His answer was transaction costs: searching for specialists, negotiating contracts, and policing quality across open markets.

  • Hiring external vendors once meant steep search & contract costs
  • Firms grew only until internal bureaucracy cost just as much

Source: Ronald Coase, 'The Nature of the Firm', Economica (1937); Nobel Prize in Economics (1991)

Chasing Headcount Status Recreates Bureaucracy

For eighty years, high transaction costs forced ambitious founders to hire big internal staffs. Today, many entrepreneurs still chase headcount for status, recreating slow corporate bureaucracy.

Automate Repeatable Work Before Hiring

Modern APIs, cloud billing, and AI workflows collapse coordination friction. Build Coasean micro-firms instead: keep core ownership tiny and automate repeatable production before hiring anyone.

Optimize Profit Per Person, Not Office Size

Measure your business by free cashflow and calm calendar hours per person, never by crowded office headcount.

  • Replace repetitive admin with deterministic code
  • Judge success by free cashflow and unscheduled hours

Source: Mind & Margin application of Ronald Coase, Economica (1937)
