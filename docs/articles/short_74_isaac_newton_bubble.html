How FOMO Cost Sir Isaac Newton a Fortune in the 1720 South Sea Bubble

In 1720, Sir Isaac Newton booked roughly a 20,000-pound profit in South Sea Company shares, then watched friends grow rich, re-entered near summer highs above 900 pounds per share, and converted safe annuities into stock, losing 10,000 to 20,000 pounds.

Newton Booked 20,000 Pounds, Then Chased the Top

In spring 1720, Sir Isaac Newton cashed out of South Sea Company stock early with roughly a twenty-thousand-pound profit, then lost a fortune chasing his friends back near the top.

  • Cashed out early in spring 1720 for ~20,000-pound gain
  • Re-entered near summer peaks above 900 pounds per share

Source: Andrew Odlyzko, Notes and Records of the Royal Society (2018)

Swapped Safe Annuities Near 900 Pounds a Share

Archival research by historian Andrew Odlyzko shows Newton watched acquaintances get rich all summer, bought back near peaks above nine hundred pounds, and swapped safe annuities, finishing fifty-four percent worse off on those annuities.

  • Watched wealthy peers multiply paper fortunes all summer
  • Lost 10,000 to 20,000 pounds when shares collapsed

Source: Andrew Odlyzko, Notes and Records of the Royal Society (2018)

Calculus Genius Cannot Outrun Social Comparison

The physicist who invented calculus and mapped planetary motion still cracked under social comparison, proving that raw analytical IQ offers zero protection once watching neighbors get rich hijacks your discipline.

Source: Mind & Margin rule based on Odlyzko, Royal Society (2018)

Enforce a 90-Day No-Chase Lockout After Selling

Defend your portfolio with a written cool-down rule: whenever you sell an overheated asset at your target valuation, forbid yourself from buying that ticker back at a higher price for ninety days.

Source: Mind & Margin protocol (advice, not a study)

Temperament Protects What Intellect Builds

Market manias punish envy far harder than ignorance, so let written exit rules guard your capital when the crowd loses its head.

  • Missing the final 50% of a bubble costs zero real cash
  • Chasing back in at the top wipes out years of savings
