Why Humans Overlook Subtraction Even When Less Works Better

Across 8 studies published in Nature in 2021, Gabrielle Adams, Leidy Klotz, and colleagues showed that people systematically overlook subtractive solutions even when removing pieces is faster and cheaper. Here is the Minus-One rule for wealth and focus.

Why Smart Brains Default to Adding More

Ask smart people to fix a wobbly Lego bridge, a cluttered schedule, or a bloated budget, and almost everyone adds more.

  • Wobbly Lego bridge? Most people buy extra bricks
  • Removing one support brick fixed it for free

Source: Adams, Converse, Hales & Klotz, Nature (2021)

Adams & Klotz's 8-Experiment 'Nature' Paper

Across eight experiments published in Nature in 2021, Gabrielle Adams, Leidy Klotz, and colleagues found humans systematically overlook subtractive changes, even when removing one piece is faster and cheaper.

  • Tested across designs, essays, recipes, and schedules
  • Subtractive fixes rarely came to mind unprompted

Source: Adams et al., 'People systematically overlook subtractive changes', Nature (2021)

Busy Brains Pile New Apps Onto Broken Systems

Under mental overload, that additive bias gets worse: we pile new productivity apps onto broken routines and stack side hustles onto leaky monthly overhead.

Taleb's Via Negativa: Delete Before You Add

Adopt Nassim Taleb's Via Negativa rule: before adding any new habit, tool, or expense, delete one recurring obligation or bill that drains your baseline energy.

Source: Nassim Nicholas Taleb, Antifragile (2012), Via Negativa

Run the Minus-One Prompt Today

Subtract friction first, and your existing focus and cashflow start compounding without a single extra moving part.

  • Ask: What can I remove to solve this for $0?
  • Zero-maintenance deletions beat fragile new routines

Source: Mind & Margin rule based on Adams et al. (2021) & Taleb (2012)
