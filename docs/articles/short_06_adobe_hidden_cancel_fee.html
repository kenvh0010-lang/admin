Why Adobe Agreed to a $150 Million Cancellation Fee Deal

On March 13, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice announced that Adobe agreed to a $150 million settlement ($75 million in civil penalties plus $75 million in free services) to resolve federal allegations over hidden 50% early termination fees on Annual, Paid Monthly subscriptions.

Adobe's $150M DOJ Settlement

In March 2026, the Justice Department announced Adobe agreed to a 150 million dollar deal over hidden subscription cancellation fees.

Source: U.S. Department of Justice Press Release (Mar 13, 2026)

Pre-Selected Annual Lock-In

Look at Example 1 from the DOJ complaint: Adobe pre-selected Annual, paid monthly, tucking the fee terms inside a tiny info icon.

Source: U.S. v. Adobe Inc., No. 5:24-cv-03630, Complaint p. 8 (N.D. Cal.)

50% of Months Remaining

Prosecutors alleged many subscribers only learned they owed 50 percent of their remaining annual contract when they tried to cancel.

Source: U.S. v. Adobe Inc., Complaint Para. 51-52, p. 11 (N.D. Cal.)

$75M Penalty + $75M Credits

Without admitting liability, Adobe agreed to pay a 75 million dollar civil penalty, provide 75 million in free services, and simplify canceling.

Source: DOJ Proposed Stipulated Order Announcement (Mar 13, 2026)

Paid Monthly Isn't Cancel Anytime

Never read paid monthly as cancel anytime. Open every tooltip and check the contract length before entering a card.

Source: Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act & DOJ Settlement (2026)
