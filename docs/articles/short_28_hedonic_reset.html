Why Every Luxury Soon Feels Normal (Hedonic Adaptation)

In 1971, psychologists Philip Brickman and Donald Campbell described why big material upgrades fade into the background while the monthly bills last for years. This video covers the Intentional Contrast protocol to beat hedonic adaptation.

When Luxury Turns Into Wallpaper

Nearly every expensive upgrade you crave right now will start to feel like background wallpaper surprisingly fast.

  • Day one brings a rush of novelty
  • Weeks later, it feels normal again

Source: Brickman & Campbell, 'Hedonic relativism and planning the good society' (1971)

Brickman & Campbell's Hedonic Treadmill

In 1971, psychologists Philip Brickman and Donald Campbell described the hedonic treadmill: people adapt to nicer apartments, newer cars, and bigger screens, and their happiness drifts back toward where it started.

  • Human nervous systems detect changes, not static comfort
  • Even major windfalls snap back toward baseline mood

Source: Brickman & Campbell (1971); Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman, lottery winners study (1978)

90 Days of Thrill for 60 Months of Bills

The financial trap is asymmetric. The dopamine rush of a luxury lease evaporates in three months, while the fixed monthly bill stays locked in for five years.

The Intentional Contrast Protocol

To escape without feeling deprived, practice Intentional Contrast: keep your monthly baseline lean, then enjoy occasional debt-free experiences that stay special because they are rare.

Quarterly 7-Day Simplicity Reset

Run a seven-day simplicity reset every quarter so ordinary comforts feel rich again and your savings compound untouched.

  • Pause non-essential spending for 1 week per quarter
  • Bank the margin and restore sensitivity to simple wins
