Why Adding More People Makes Late Projects Later

In his classic 1975 book 'The Mythical Man-Month', IBM engineering manager Fred Brooks explained why adding people to a late project delays it further: communication channels explode by N*(N-1)/2. Here is why cutting scope beats adding headcount.

Why Extra Help Pushes Deadlines Further Away

When a critical software or creative project falls behind schedule, adding three helpful teammates almost always pushes the finish line even further away.

  • Project slips behind -> managers add 3 extra people
  • Delivery slows down instead of speeding up

Source: Fred Brooks, The Mythical Man-Month (1975)

Communication Channels Explode by N(N-1)/2

In his 1975 classic The Mythical Man-Month, IBM engineering manager Fred Brooks showed why: communication channels scale by N times N minus one over two. Three builders have three links; ten create forty-five.

  • 3 builders = only 3 communication links to align
  • 10 people = 45 links • 15 people = 105 links

Source: Fred Brooks, The Mythical Man-Month (1975)

Onboarding Steals Hours From Your Top Builders

Every newcomer requires onboarding from your busiest builders right when time is scarcest, turning productive creation hours into status calls, handoffs, and merge conflicts.

Cut Non-Essential Scope, Never Add Chaos

When a deadline slips, resist throwing headcount at the problem. Cut non-essential scope, shield a tiny two-to-three-person pod from meetings, and ship the core asset first.

Small Aligned Pods Beat Crowded Committees

Shrink scope before expanding headcount, because tiny aligned pods out-ship crowded committees every single quarter.

  • Simplify Version 1.0 down to what truly matters
  • Protect small ownership pods from calendar bloat

Source: Mind & Margin execution rule based on Fred Brooks (1975)
