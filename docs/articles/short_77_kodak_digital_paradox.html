Kodak Invented the Digital Camera in 1975 and Still Went Bankrupt

In December 1975, 24-year-old Kodak engineer Steven Sasson built the world's first 8-pound digital camera (U.S. Patent 4,131,919). Fearful of cannibalizing high-margin chemical film that peaked near $16 billion in 1996, Kodak hesitated until filing Chapter 11 bankruptcy on January 19, 2012.

Kodak Built the First Digital Camera in December 1975

Eastman Kodak did not miss the digital photography wave; a twenty-four-year-old Kodak electrical engineer named Steven Sasson built the world's first working eight-pound digital camera in December 1975.

  • 24-year-old Kodak engineer captured filmless images onto tape
  • Awarded U.S. Patent 4,131,919 decades before smartphones

Source: U.S. Patent 4,131,919 (1978); Eastman Kodak Chapter 11 SDNY (Jan 19, 2012)

Protected $16B Film Margins Until 2012 Bankruptcy

Sasson's camera earned U.S. Patent 4,131,919, yet executives feared cannibalizing lucrative chemical film. Kodak revenue peaked near sixteen billion dollars in 1996 before digital rivals crushed it into Chapter 11 bankruptcy on January 19, 2012.

  • Patented digital tech (4,131,919) yet feared hurting film profits
  • Revenue peaked near $16B (1996) -> Chapter 11 on Jan 19, 2012

Source: U.S. Patent 4,131,919 (1978); Eastman Kodak Chapter 11 SDNY (Jan 19, 2012)

Refusing to Cannibalize Never Stops the Future

Locking a breakthrough inside a corporate drawer never halts technological shifts; protecting yesterday's comfortable margin only guarantees that hungry outsiders collect tomorrow's entire market instead of you.
