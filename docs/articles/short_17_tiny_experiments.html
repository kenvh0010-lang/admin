Why Rigid 5-Year Plans Backfire (Run 'Tiny Experiments' Instead)

Rigid 5-year career ladders break in fast-moving markets. Neuroscientist Anne-Laure Le Cunff's Tiny Experiments framework replaces high-pressure life plans with 14-day curiosity pacts that compound real-world data.

Why Linear 5-Year Plans Create Paralysis

The fastest way to stall your momentum in a fast-changing world is locking yourself into a rigid five-year life plan.

  • Predicting life 60 months ahead locks in outdated guesses
  • High stakes trigger perfectionism before you even start

Neuroscientist Anne-Laure Le Cunff wrote about why ambitious planners burn out chasing fixed ladders. In her book Tiny Experiments, she argues that we learn faster from short feedback loops than from distant finish lines.

  • Ambitious brains burn out chasing static linear ladders
  • Short experimental cycles turn anxiety into curiosity

High-Stakes Goals vs Clean Lab Data

A massive five-year goal frames every detour as personal failure. A tiny experiment frames every result as clean laboratory data. You stop asking if something works forever and test what happens over two weeks.

Write One 14-Day Curiosity Pact

Use the Curiosity Pact: write down 'I will perform one specific action for fourteen days.' Publish ten short notes, test one workflow, or wake thirty minutes early, then inspect the data.

26 Real Experiments Beat 1 Spreadsheet

Twenty-six two-week experiments in a year can show you paths no spreadsheet would predict.

  • Replace 'What is my passion?' with 'What is my next 14-day test?'
  • Keep what energizes you and drop what drains you guilt-free

Source: Calculation: 52 weeks / 2 = 26 experiments
