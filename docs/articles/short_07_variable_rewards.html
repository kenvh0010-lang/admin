Why Casinos & Apps Use 'Variable Rewards' Against Your Wallet

In 1957, Charles Ferster and B.F. Skinner showed that unpredictable rewards keep behavior going longer than predictable ones. This video shows how shopping apps use the same trick on your wallet, and how to add friction back.

B.F. Skinner's Variable Ratio Discovery

In 1957, Charles Ferster and B.F. Skinner found that pigeons rewarded on a fixed schedule pecked in bursts, then paused. With rewards after an unpredictable number of pecks, they pecked fast and kept going.

  • Fixed schedule: pecking came in bursts with pauses
  • Random schedule: fast, steady pecking that was hard to stop

Source: Ferster & Skinner, Schedules of Reinforcement (1957)

Uncertainty Keeps You Chasing

Brain research from Wolfram Schultz's lab found dopamine activity builds most when the odds of a reward are fifty-fifty. Flash-sale countdowns, mystery discounts, and pull-to-refresh feeds use that same uncertainty.

Source: Fiorillo, Tobler & Schultz, Science (2003)
