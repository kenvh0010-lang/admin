How Visible Proof Commands Premium Prices: The Market for Lemons

Nobel laureates George Akerlof (1970) and Michael Spence (1973) showed why hidden quality forces buyers to discount every offer like a defective 'lemon', unless sellers provide costly, hard-to-fake proof of work.

Why New Cars Drop Thousands Driving Off the Lot

Why does a pristine used car lose thousands in value the minute it leaves the lot? Because buyers cannot verify hidden quality.

  • Seller knows if the engine is sound; buyer does not
  • Uncertainty forces rational buyers to slash their bid

Source: George A. Akerlof, Quarterly Journal of Economics (1970)

Akerlof's Lemons (1970) & Spence's Signals (1973)

Economist George Akerlof explained why in his 1970 Market for Lemons paper: when buyers cannot separate great peaches from defective lemons, they discount everyone. In 1973, Michael Spence showed only costly, hard-to-fake signals restore premium prices.

  • Akerlof (1970): unverified markets price everything toward lemons
  • Spence (1973): only hard-to-fake signals separate top talent

Source: Akerlof, QJE (1970); Michael Spence, 'Job Market Signaling', QJE (1973)

AI Made Verbal Claims Zero-Cost 'Cheap Talk'

In an AI-flooded job and freelance market, polished resumes and buzzwords cost zero effort to fake, so skeptical clients price verbal promises like lemons.

Publish Artifacts Fakers Cannot Copy

Ship public proof of work that fakers cannot copy: live teardowns, working code repositories, or documented client audits proving your exact thinking before pitching.

Remove Buyer Doubt to Earn Peach Prices

Eliminate the buyer's uncertainty with visible proof, and you command peach pricing in a noisy market.

  • Stop telling buyers you are detail-oriented; show an audit
  • Public proof sells quietly 24 hours a day

Source: Mind & Margin rule based on Akerlof (1970) & Spence (1973)
