Slow Productivity: Do Fewer Things and Finish What Matters

Asana's 2021 Anatomy of Work survey found people spend about 60% of their time on 'work about work': status updates, searching, and chasing approvals. Drawing on Cal Newport's Slow Productivity, Sophie Leroy's Attention Residue, and 90-minute focus blocks, here is a calmer way to finish real work.

The 60% 'Work About Work' Paradox

Asana's 2021 Anatomy of Work survey of more than ten thousand workers found that about sixty percent of their time went to work about work: status threads, calendar shuffling, chasing approvals, and searching for information. Millions of smart people finish Friday evening drained, yet struggle to point to one lasting thing they actually finished all week.

  • Asana (2021): ~60% of time goes to 'work about work'
  • Real craftsmanship gets squeezed into the leftovers
  • Exhaustion grows while finished work stalls

Source: Asana, Anatomy of Work Global Index (2021)

Why Offices Mistake Visible Franticness for Value

Georgetown computer science professor Cal Newport traces this burnout epidemic to a structural flaw he calls Pseudo-Productivity. On twentieth-century factory floors, supervisors could count physical widgets coming off a belt every hour. When knowledge work arrived without an obvious hourly widget count, workplaces lazily substituted visible activity as a proxy for effort. Keeping a green chat dot lit for ten hours, answering emails in ninety seconds, and sitting in back-to-back meetings looks productive while starving every project that actually matters.

Why Juggling 8 Projects Drains Your Focus

Research explains why overloaded task lists collapse. Professor Sophie Leroy showed that when you switch away from an unfinished task, part of your attention stays stuck on it. She called this attention residue. Separately, Gloria Mark's research found it takes about twenty-three minutes on average to get back to an interrupted task. Juggle eight active projects, and each one sends its own stream of check-ins all day.

  • Sophie Leroy: unfinished tasks leave attention residue
  • Gloria Mark: ~23 minutes on average to resume an interrupted task

Source: Sophie Leroy, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes (2009); Gloria Mark et al., 'The Cost of Interrupted Work', CHI (2008)

Principles 1 & 2: Hard Caps and 90-Minute Rhythms

Slow Productivity flips that broken math using two foundational shifts: Do Fewer Things, and Work at a Natural Pace. Capping your major commitments at two or three at a time cuts the check-ins and handoffs that eat your week, freeing energy to actually finish work. Working at a natural pace borrows from physiologist Nathaniel Kleitman's idea of ninety-minute rest-activity cycles: pair focused sprints with real recovery, and pace big goals across calm seasons instead of all-nighters.

Source: Cal Newport, Slow Productivity (2024); Nathaniel Kleitman, basic rest-activity cycle

Principle 3: Craftsmanship Buys Rare Autonomy

Newport's third principle locks the entire system together: Obsess Over Quality. In both careers and solo media businesses, shipping ten rushed, average deliverables generates zero lasting leverage, whereas producing one undeniably thoughtful flagship asset compounds reputation and demand for years. Better yet, signature quality earns you immunity from office theater, colleagues and audiences gladly forgive a slower afternoon email reply when the finished work you deliver solves problems at a level nobody else can touch.

Install the 3-Rule Slow Productivity OS

Deploy this operating system tomorrow morning using three concrete rules. First, replace your endless to-do list with a Two-List Pull System: park incoming requests inside a Holding Tank, and enforce a strict limit of three items on your Active List, nothing new gets pulled in until one active project ships. Second, protect one sealed ninety-minute Ultradian Deep Block every morning with your phone in another room before opening email. Third, batch all messages, logistics, and admin into two crisp twenty-five-minute windows at midday and four P.M.

  • 1. Two-List Pull System: Holding Tank vs max 3 Active commitments
  • 2. Sealed 90-Minute Morning Ultradian Block (phone in another room)
  • 3. Two 25-Minute Comms Windows (11:30 AM & 4:00 PM) for all pings
