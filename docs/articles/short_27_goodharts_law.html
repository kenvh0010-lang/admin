The Cobra Effect: When Chasing Metrics Ruins Your Life

As the story goes, when colonial officials paid a bounty for dead cobras in Delhi, people started breeding cobras for the cash. This video shows how Goodhart's Law explains why chasing vanity metrics backfires.

How a Snake Bounty Multiplied Snakes

As the story goes, when colonial officials in Delhi offered cash for every dead cobra, the city ended up with even more venomous snakes.

  • Officials paid cash for every dead cobra skin delivered
  • Incentives quietly rewired human behavior overnight

From Backyard Snake Farms to Goodhart's Law

Locals started breeding cobras in backyard sheds just to collect the payout. When officials discovered the scheme and canceled the bounty, breeders released the worthless snakes into the streets.

  • Enterprising locals bred cobras indoors for steady payouts
  • Canceled bounty -> worthless snakes released into Delhi

When Measures Become Targets, They Break

In 1975, British economist Charles Goodhart observed that a number used as a target stops measuring what it used to. Anthropologist Marilyn Strathern later put it simply: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.

Audit Your Personal Vanity Metrics

You fall into the same trap whenever you chase hours logged at a desk, app streaks, or vanity views instead of real skills and cashflow margin.

Use Numbers as a Compass, Never an Idol

Use numbers as a quiet compass, never as your identity, because the second you worship the scoreboard you start gaming yourself.

  • Ask: could I hit this number while getting worse?
  • Reward lasting output over cosmetic dashboards
