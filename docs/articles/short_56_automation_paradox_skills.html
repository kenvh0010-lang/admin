Lisanne Bainbridge's Ironies of Automation

In 1983, psychologist Lisanne Bainbridge published Ironies of Automation in the journal Automatica. When machines take over ninety-five percent of routine work, human skill rusts right before the hardest five-percent emergency.

  • Machines absorb the easy 95% that kept human instincts sharp
  • Rusty humans are then expected to rescue rare 5% crises

Source: Lisanne Bainbridge, Automatica, Vol. 19, No. 6, pp. 775-779 (1983)

From Sharp Architect to Passive Rubber-Stamper

Outsourcing every first draft, calculation, and diagnosis turns sharp professionals into passive rubber-stampers who can no longer spot a confident AI error.

Log 10 Manual Minutes Before Every Prompt

Fly manually first: spend ten quiet minutes sketching your own logic, numbers, or structure before asking AI to challenge or accelerate your draft.

Source: Mind & Margin protocol based on Bainbridge (1983) & Lee et al., CHI (2025)

Automate Drudgery, Never Your Core Judgment

Automate repetitive execution drudgery, never the raw cognitive reps that keep your domain judgment sharp when automated systems fail.

  • Offload formatting, boilerplate, and tedious data cleanup
  • Keep problem framing and verification strictly in your hands
