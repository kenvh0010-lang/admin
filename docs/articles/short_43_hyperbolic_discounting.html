Why Saving for Retirement Feels Like Paying a Stranger

To your brain, saving money for yourself thirty years from now feels neurologically similar to handing your paycheck to a total stranger.

  • Present desires feel vivid, urgent, and personal
  • 70-year-old you registers like somebody else entirely

Source: Ersner-Hershfield et al., SCAN (2009); Hershfield et al., JMR (2011)

Hal Hershfield's Stanford Future-Self Experiments

Psychologist Hal Hershfield and Stanford colleagues showed via brain scans that picturing your distant future self barely activates self-referential regions. In their 2011 Journal of Marketing Research study, seeing age-progressed portraits doubled retirement contributions.

  • fMRI scans showed muted self-recognition for distant future selves
  • Viewing realistic aged avatars doubled hypothetical retirement saving

Source: Hershfield et al., 'Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings of the Future Self', JMR (2011)
