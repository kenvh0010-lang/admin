Why Loyalty Cards With 2 Free Stamps Finish Faster (Goal Gradient)

Building on Clark Hull's 1932 Goal-Gradient Hypothesis, marketing researchers Ran Kivetz, Oleg Urminsky, and Yuhuang Zheng proved in 2006 that a 12-stamp card with 2 free stamps completed at 34% versus 19% for an identical 10-stamp card.

12 Stamps Minus 2 Beat 10 Blank Stamps

Why did a twelve-stamp loyalty card with two free stamps nearly double completion over a blank ten-stamp card requiring identical purchases?

  • Card A: 10 empty boxes -> 19% finished
  • Card B: 12 boxes with 2 pre-stamped -> 34% finished

Source: Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, Journal of Marketing Research (2006)

Hull (1932) & Kivetz, Urminsky, Zheng (2006)

Psychologist Clark Hull noticed in 1932 that effort speeds up near a finish line. In 2006, Ran Kivetz, Oleg Urminsky, and Yuhuang Zheng proved illusory head starts lifted car-wash card completion from nineteen percent to thirty-four.

  • Clark Hull (1932): subjects speed up near visible finish lines
  • Kivetz et al. (2006): starting at 17% triggered faster repeat visits

Source: Clark Hull, Psych. Review (1932); Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, JMR (2006)

Zero Percent Kills Good Projects in Week One

Starting a new savings fund or digital project at zero percent feels miles from done, causing most beginners to stall in week one.

Pre-Stamp Steps 1 and 2 on Every Sprint

Pre-stamp your own milestones: break big goals into ten-step sprints, and log account setup and outline notes as steps one and two immediately.

Start Every Card Already in Motion

Never stare at zero percent when a twenty-percent head start pulls your brain across the finish line.

  • Shrink distant 12-month goals into 10-box cards
  • Let the Goal-Gradient pull you through the final 8
