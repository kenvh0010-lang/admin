Why 'Good Enough' Decision-Makers End Up Happier

Nobel laureate Herbert Simon coined 'satisficing' in 1956, and psychologist Barry Schwartz proved in a 2002 Journal of Personality and Social Psychology study that relentless maximizers feel worse even after winning objectively good outcomes. Here is when 'good enough' wins.

Why 50 Open Browser Tabs Leave You Less Happy

Why do people who spend four hours comparing fifty nearly identical hotels end up less happy than decisive travelers who pick in five minutes?

  • Four hours spent comparing nearly identical options
  • Less satisfaction after buying than fast deciders

Source: Barry Schwartz et al., Journal of Personality and Social Psychology (2002)

Herbert Simon (1956) & Barry Schwartz (2002)

Nobel laureate Herbert Simon coined satisficing in 1956 for choosing the first option that meets clear standards. In 2002, psychologist Barry Schwartz and colleagues proved relentless maximizers experience higher regret and lower satisfaction.

  • Simon (1956): 'satisfice' = pick first option meeting your bar
  • Schwartz et al. (2002): maximizers suffer chronic buyer's remorse

Source: Herbert Simon, Psychological Review (1956); Schwartz et al., JPSP (2002)

Spending Deep-Work Energy on Reversible Trivia

Maximizing reversible purchases drains hours of precious focus. Even after buying a top-rated laptop, maximizers torture themselves wondering whether tab forty-seven held a slightly better deal.

Set 3 Criteria and a 10-Minute Timer

Reserve exhaustive research for rare, irreversible life bets. For everyday tools and purchases, define three must-have criteria, set a ten-minute timer, and pick the first match.

Protect Bandwidth for Decisions That Compound

Satisfice small reversible choices quickly so your best mental energy stays locked on high-leverage work.

  • Close comparison tabs once your baseline bar is met
  • Invest reclaimed hours into building durable skills and assets

Source: Mind & Margin decision rule based on Simon (1956) and Schwartz et al. (2002)
