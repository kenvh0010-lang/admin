Why Apple's Third Co-Founder Sold 10% for $800 After 12 Days

On April 1, 1976, Ronald Wayne typed Apple Computer's original partnership agreement for a 10% stake alongside Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak. Twelve days later, fearing unlimited partnership debt liability, he sold his 10% for $800.

Apple's Third Co-Founder Cashed Out for $800

On April 1, 1976, forty-one-year-old draftsman Ronald Wayne typed Apple Computer's partnership contract for a ten percent stake, then sold his entire share twelve days later for eight hundred dollars, plus fifteen hundred later.

  • Typed Apple's April 1, 1976 contract for a 10% stake
  • Withdrew April 12 for $800, plus $1,500 later that year

Source: Apple Computer Partnership Withdrawal (Apr 12, 1976); Ronald Wayne (2011)

Why Unlimited Liability Scared Only One Partner

Early Apple was a general partnership carrying unlimited personal liability. Young Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak owned almost nothing creditors could seize, whereas Wayne held personal assets and remembered paying off debts from a failed slot-machine venture.

Source: Apple Computer Partnership Withdrawal (Apr 12, 1976); Ronald Wayne (2011)

Unshielded Downside Forces Early Exits

Once Jobs ordered parts on credit, Wayne knew unpaid supplier bills could legally drain his personal savings. Unshielded downside risk forces rational people out of generational upside.

  • Jobs took credit parts orders that exposed Wayne's savings
  • Uncapped downside forces smart operators out of big upside

Source: Apple Computer Partnership Withdrawal (Apr 12, 1976); Ronald Wayne (2011)

Ring-Fence Liability So You Can Stay in the Game

Ring-fence every asymmetric venture inside capped legal liability, refuse personal debt guarantees, and fund high-upside bets strictly with surplus cash your household bills never require.

Source: Mind & Margin rule based on Apple Partnership Withdrawal (1976)

Cap Your Ruin Before Chasing Asymmetric Returns

Cap your personal ruin upfront, because you can only hold ten-year compounders when worst-case losses cannot touch your survival.

  • Separate business risk from family housing and cash reserves
  • Size volatile bets so a zero never changes your lifestyle
