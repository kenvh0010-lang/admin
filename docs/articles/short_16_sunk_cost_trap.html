The 'Concorde Fallacy' Keeping You in Dead-End Projects

Britain and France kept funding the supersonic Concorde long after it was clear it would never pay back its costs. This video shows how the Concorde Fallacy traps ambitious builders and the Zero-Base Question to escape it.

Why High Achievers Stay Too Long

Why do smart people stay in draining careers, dead-end side projects, and broken commitments for years after they clearly stop working?

  • Intelligent people rarely struggle to start hard things
  • They struggle to walk away once an investment stops paying

The 1976 Supersonic Money Pit

Biologists Richard Dawkins and Tamsin Carlisle named it the Concorde Fallacy in 1976, the year the supersonic jet entered service. Britain and France kept funding Concorde long after it was clear it would never pay back its costs.

  • Concorde was never expected to repay its development costs
  • Both governments kept paying so past spending would not look wasted

Throwing Future Margin After Past Pride

Your brain hates admitting a loss so intensely that it throws precious future years after unrecoverable past years. Past time and past money are gone forever.

Ask the Zero-Base Entry Question

Break the spell with the Zero-Base Question: knowing everything I know today, if I were not already inside this project or commitment right now, would I enter it from scratch?

Walking Away Early Is Capital Allocation

If the honest answer is no, walk away. That frees your time and money for something better.

  • Never pay future life energy to defend a past mistake
  • Audit your top 3 commitments every quarter from Day 0
