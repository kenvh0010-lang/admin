Mandelbrot & Taleb's Lindy Effect

Mathematician Benoit Mandelbrot formalized it and author Nassim Taleb popularized it as the Lindy Effect: for non-perishable things like ideas and skills, the longer something has lasted, the longer it is likely to last.

  • Non-perishable ideas age in reverse: survival predicts longevity
  • Every century a skill endures proves permanent demand

Source: Nassim Nicholas Taleb, Antifragile (2012), the Lindy Effect

Expiring Tactics vs Compounding Foundations

Chasing shiny app workflows puts you on a treadmill that resets every two years. Mastering clear writing, behavioral persuasion, systems design, and capital allocation compounds for life.

The 80/20 Lindy Learning Portfolio

Use the eighty-twenty Lindy rule: spend twenty percent of your learning budget on modern AI tools, and eighty percent sharpening long-tested two-thousand-year-old foundations.
