Why Earning More Still Keeps You Broke (The Diderot Trap)

In 1765, French philosopher Denis Diderot got rich overnight—and went broke within months because of one scarlet robe. Here is how the Diderot Effect silently drains modern paychecks and the 72-hour rule to stop it.

Why Getting a Raise Makes People Poorer

Sixty-two percent of people earning six figures still live paycheck to paycheck. And a two-hundred-fifty-year-old story explains why.

  • Higher income rarely fixes money stress
  • A 250-year-old psychological chain reaction explains why

Denis Diderot & The Scarlet Robe (1765)

In 1765, philosopher Denis Diderot was gifted a luxurious scarlet robe. Next to that robe, his old wooden desk looked cheap. So he replaced the desk. Then the rug. Then every chair in his home.

  • Gifted one luxury scarlet dressing gown
  • Suddenly his desk, rug, and chairs looked 'cheap'

One Upgrade Triggers Five Hidden Costs

Psychologists call this the Diderot Effect. You rarely buy just one upgrade—you buy a whole new identity that demands five more purchases to match.

The 50% Invisible Raise Rule

To break the spiral, use the Invisible Raise Rule: whenever your income jumps, automatically route fifty percent into assets before it ever hits your checking account.

Wealth Is What You Don't Spend

Real wealth isn't what people see in your driveway—it's the freedom you didn't trade away. Which is exactly why...

  • Wait 72 hours before matching a new purchase
  • Keep the raise—skip the chain reaction
