Why Getting a Raise Makes People Poorer

Nearly half of Americans earning over one hundred thousand dollars a year live paycheck to paycheck. A story from 1769 explains why.

  • Higher income rarely fixes money stress

Denis Diderot & The Scarlet Robe (1769)

In 1769, the philosopher Denis Diderot wrote about a luxurious scarlet robe he had been given. Next to that robe, his old desk looked cheap. So he replaced the desk. Then the tapestry. Then the chairs.

  • Gifted one luxury scarlet dressing gown
  • Suddenly his desk, rug, and chairs looked 'cheap'

Source: Denis Diderot, 'Regrets on Parting with My Old Dressing Gown' (1769)

One Upgrade Triggers Five Hidden Costs

The anthropologist Grant McCracken later named this the Diderot Effect. You rarely buy just one upgrade. You buy a new identity that demands more purchases to match.

Source: Grant McCracken, Culture and Consumption (1988)

The 50% Invisible Raise Rule

To break the spiral, use the Invisible Raise Rule: whenever your income jumps, automatically route fifty percent into assets before it ever hits your checking account.

Source: Mind & Margin rule of thumb (advice, not a study)

Wealth Is What You Don't Spend

Real wealth isn't what people see in your driveway, it's the freedom you didn't trade away.

  • Wait 72 hours before matching a new purchase
  • Keep the raise, skip the chain reaction
