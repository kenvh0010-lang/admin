Why the $149 Tier Exists Only to Sell You the $79 Plan

That overpriced $149 top tier on software and subscription pages is rarely meant to be bought, it is an anchor planted to make $79 feel cheap. Here is MIT economist Dan Ariely's Decoy Effect study and the Blank-Sheet Rule.

The Overpriced Tier Nobody Is Meant to Buy

Have you ever noticed a pricing page with an overpriced hundred-forty-nine-dollar tier that almost nobody needs? You are not meant to buy it, you are meant to measure against it.

  • Top pricing tiers often exist purely as psychological contrast
  • You measure the middle option against the anchor, not reality

Source: Illustration (example prices)

Dan Ariely's Economist Subscription Experiment

Behavioral economist Dan Ariely tested this with Economist subscription offers in Predictably Irrational. With a print-only decoy at the same price as print plus web, eighty-four percent of students chose the expensive bundle. Without the decoy, only thirty-two percent did.

  • With a decoy option, 84% picked the expensive bundle
  • Remove the decoy, and only 32% chose the premium plan

Source: Dan Ariely, Predictably Irrational (2008), chapter 1

Relative Bargains vs Absolute Utility

Psychologists call this Price Anchoring. Your brain struggles to calculate standalone value, so sellers plant a high anchor first to make seventy-nine dollars feel cheap.

Run the Blank-Sheet Ceiling Test

Use the Blank-Sheet Test before any purchase: cover the pricing table, write down what solving that exact problem is worth to you, and reject anything above it.

Fake Discounts Still Drain Real Margin

A steep discount off a fake anchor is still a real leak.

  • Crossed-out MSRPs and extreme tiers are comparison traps
  • Set your walk-away price before looking at the menu
