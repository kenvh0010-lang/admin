How One Unupdated Server Cost $460 Million in 45 Minutes

According to SEC Release No. 34-70694, Knight Capital deployed new trading code manually to only seven of eight servers on August 1, 2012. In 45 minutes, dead 2003 code executed over 4 million trades and lost more than $460 million.

$460 Million Gone in 45 Minutes

One missed server update cost a Wall Street trading giant over 460 million dollars in 45 minutes.

Source: SEC Release No. 34-70694, In the Matter of Knight Capital (Oct 16, 2013)

Updated 7 of 8 Servers

A technician manually copied new code to seven servers, missed the eighth, and accidentally woke up dead 2003 code called Power Peg.

Source: SEC Order paras. 15-16, p. 6

4 Million Trades in 154 Stocks

At market open, that server fired millions of orders, racking up 4 million executions across 397 million shares before engineers pulled the plug.

Source: SEC Order para. 1, p. 2

97 Unread Alerts & $12M Fine

An internal system had sent 97 Power Peg emails before the open, but nobody treated them as alerts. Knight later paid a 12 million dollar SEC fine.

Source: SEC Order para. 19, p. 6 & Section IV.C, p. 18

Delete Dead Code. Build Kill Switches.

Any automated machine moving real money needs two guardrails: zero manual server deploys, and an automatic kill switch.

Source: SEC Market Access Rule 15c3-5 Findings
