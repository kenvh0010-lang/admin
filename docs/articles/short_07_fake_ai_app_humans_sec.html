This $42M 'AI' Shopping App Was Secretly Hundreds of Humans

In April 2025, the U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York and the SEC charged the former CEO of shopping app startup Nate, alleging the company raised over $42 million on claims of autonomous AI checkout while contractors in the Philippines and Romania manually placed orders.

Raised $42M for 'AI' Checkout

Federal prosecutors and the SEC charged the former CEO of shopping app Nate after raising over 42 million dollars on claims of autonomous AI.

Source: U.S. Attorney's Office S.D.N.Y. & SEC LR-26282 (Apr 2025)

Claimed 93% to 97% AI Success

The indictment alleges investors were told proprietary neural networks finished checkouts in seconds with 93 to 97 percent accuracy.

Source: U.S. v. Albert Saniger Indictment Para. 5, p. 3 (S.D.N.Y.)

Humans Typed Orders Overseas

Prosecutors allege the app's actual AI automation rate was effectively zero, and hundreds of contractors in the Philippines and Romania placed orders manually.

Source: U.S. v. Saniger Indictment Count One, p. 1 (S.D.N.Y.)

Scripts, Not Autonomous AI

Even its limited bots allegedly used site-specific scripts, not AI. These are indictment charges, and the defendant is presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

Source: U.S. v. Saniger Indictment Para. 7-8, p. 4 (S.D.N.Y.)

Audit Live Inputs, Not Demos

Never trust a rehearsed AI demo when money is on the line. Test the product live with your own messy, unscripted inputs.

Source: SEC Litigation Release No. 26282 (Apr 10, 2025)
