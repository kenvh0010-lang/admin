Jeff Bezos' Regret Minimization Framework in 50 Seconds

In 1994, Jeff Bezos walked away from a lucrative Wall Street bonus at D.E. Shaw using one mental time machine: the Age-80 Regret Minimization Framework, backed by Cornell psychology research on lifelong regret.

The 1994 Wall Street Bonus Dilemma

In 1994, a thirty-year-old Wall Street vice president had to decide whether to give up a massive mid-year bonus to sell books on an obscure new network called the internet.

  • Age 30 VP at Wall Street firm D.E. Shaw
  • Quitting mid-year meant forfeiting a huge annual bonus

Project Your Mind Forward to Age 80

His boss warned him not to leave a great job. So Jeff Bezos pictured himself at age eighty looking back on his life, aiming to minimize the total regrets he would carry.

  • Step outside short-term salary noise and social approval
  • Minimize total lifelong regrets from your 80th birthday

Trying & Failing vs Never Trying at All

At eighty, he knew he would never regret missing one Wall Street bonus, and he would not even regret trying and failing. The one thing that would haunt him forever was never trying.

75% of Deep Life Regrets Are Inactions

Cornell psychologist Thomas Gilovich confirmed why in 1995: seventy-five percent of enduring human regrets come from paths we were too cautious to test, never attempts that fell short.
