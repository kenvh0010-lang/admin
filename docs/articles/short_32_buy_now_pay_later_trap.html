Stanford's 'Pain of Paying' Brain Scans

Stanford brain-imaging studies found that seeing a full price tag activates the insula, the exact neural region that processes physical pain, acting as a natural brake on impulse buying.

  • Upfront cash prices trigger healthy neural friction
  • That discomfort evolved to stop bad impulse trades

Payment Decoupling Multiplies Micro-Bills

Buy-now-pay-later buttons use what researchers call payment decoupling: the cost feels separate from the purchase. Shrinking seventy-six dollars into nineteen today makes the price feel smaller than it is.

Source: Drazen Prelec & George Loewenstein, 'The Red and the Black', Marketing Science (1998)
