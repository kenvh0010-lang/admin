How a Gas Station Janitor Built $8 Million

In 2014, Brattleboro, Vermont janitor Ronald Read died at 92 and left an estate worth about $8 million, while Harvard-educated executive Richard Fuscone went bankrupt. This video covers the lesson from Morgan Housel's 'The Psychology of Money'.

The 92-Year-Old Janitor Worth $8 Million

In 2014, a ninety-two-year-old janitor from Brattleboro, Vermont died and surprised his town with an estate worth about eight million dollars.

  • Worked at a gas station for 25 years and swept floors for 17
  • Even people close to him had no idea he was a multimillionaire

Source: Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money (2020), chapter 1

Vermont Janitor vs Harvard Executive

Ronald Read fixed cars at a gas station and swept department-store floors for forty-two years. Meanwhile, Harvard-educated executive Richard Fuscone retired early into an eighteen-thousand-square-foot mansion and declared bankruptcy after the two thousand eight crash.

Source: Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money (2020), chapter 1

High IQ Fails When Ego Forces a Sale

In Morgan Housel's The Psychology of Money, the contrast proves a hard truth: finance is the one arena where calm behavior beats raw IQ buried under debt.

  • Genius paired with lifestyle burn breaks in a downturn
  • Finance rewards calm endurance over credentials

Source: Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money (2020), chapter 1

Never Interrupt a 40-Year Horizon

Read never earned a huge salary or used risky leverage. He simply saved small margins consistently and let uninterrupted compounding run for over four decades.

Endurance Is the Ultimate Edge

Protect a survival margin you never have to sell, because ordinary patience beats reckless brilliance every time.

  • Keep fixed bills low so downturns never force panic
  • Let decades do the heavy lifting ego cannot

Source: Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money (2020), chapter 1
