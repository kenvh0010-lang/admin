How One 'If-Then' Sentence Doubled Follow-Through in Labs

Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer introduced Implementation Intentions in 1999. In a 2002 British Journal of Health Psychology trial by Milne, Orbell, and Sheeran, adding one written 'When and Where' plan lifted exercise follow-through from 38% to 91%.

38% vs 91% Follow-Through From One Sentence

How did one group of British students jump from thirty-eight percent exercise follow-through to ninety-one percent? One written If-Then sentence.

  • Control group stayed stuck at 38% completion
  • One written If-Then trigger pushed action to 91%

Source: Milne, Orbell & Sheeran, British Journal of Health Psychology (2002)

Gollwitzer (1999) & Milne, Orbell, Sheeran (2002)

Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer formalized Implementation Intentions in 1999. Testing it in 2002, Sarah Milne, Sheina Orbell, and Paschal Sheeran found scary health warnings alone stayed stuck at thirty-five percent, while specifying exact time and place hit ninety-one.

  • Health-risk pep talks alone added zero follow-through
  • Specifying exact day, time, and gym hit 91%

Source: Gollwitzer, American Psychologist (1999); Milne, Orbell & Sheeran (2002)

Vague Wishes Collapse When You Feel Tired

Vague goals like 'save more' or 'build this weekend' collapse at five PM because a tired brain has to negotiate when and where.

Pre-Code Your Cue Before Going to Sleep

Pre-load the cue tonight using Gollwitzer's exact syntax: 'If it is seven AM Saturday at my desk, then I immediately draft for ninety minutes.'

Fire Before Your Excuses Wake Up

Delegate the starting pistol to a concrete time and place so action fires before excuses wake up.

  • Never leave tomorrow's deep block unscheduled
  • Bind every core habit to a clock and a chair
