The 5 Types of Wealth: Why Financially Rich People Still Feel Broke

Optimizing exclusively for Financial Wealth silently bankrupts the four other currencies that create a rich life. Inspired by Sahil Bloom's 'The 5 Types of Wealth' and Harvard's 85-year study, This video shows how to balance Time, Social, Mental, Physical, and Financial margin.

The Trap of a One-Number Scoreboard

From your very first paycheck, modern culture hands you a remarkably narrow scoreboard: one single number inside a bank account. That single-metric obsession explains one of the strangest paradoxes of modern success, how someone can hit seven figures on paper yet wake up exhausted, isolated, and chained to a calendar they resent. As writer Sahil Bloom highlights in The 5 Types of Wealth, a genuinely rich life actually runs on five distinct currencies: Time Wealth, Social Wealth, Mental Wealth, Physical Wealth, and Financial Wealth.

  • Society trains ambitious builders to measure only Financial Wealth
  • Winning one scoreboard while losing four others still feels like poverty
  • True wealth balances Time, Social, Mental, Physical, and Financial margin

Source: Sahil Bloom, The 5 Types of Wealth (2025)

Calendar Autonomy and the 85-Year Harvard Study

Start with Time Wealth and Social Wealth. Time Wealth is not merely retiring at sixty-five; it is daily autonomy over what you work on, where you sit, and whom you build alongside. Writer Tim Urban ran the numbers: by the time a child turns eighteen, most of the in-person time a parent will ever spend with them has already happened. Meanwhile, the Harvard Study of Adult Development, running since 1938, found that how satisfied people were with their relationships at fifty predicted their health at eighty better than their cholesterol levels did.

Source: Tim Urban, 'The Tail End', Wait But Why (2015); Robert Waldinger & Marc Schulz, The Good Life (2023)

The Leading '1' Before Every Financial Zero

Next come Mental Wealth and Physical Wealth. Mental Wealth combines curiosity, purpose, and inner peace, the rare capacity to sit in a silent room for thirty minutes without drowning in status anxiety or reaching for a glowing screen. Physical Wealth is daily vitality, restorative sleep, strength, and mobility across decades. Think of Physical and Mental Wealth as the leading digit 'one' standing in front of every financial zero you accumulate. Stack six zeros behind a healthy one and you hold a million; erase that leading one and every zero behind it collapses to nothing.

  • Mental Wealth: curiosity, purpose, and calm inner space
  • Physical Wealth: sleep, strength, stamina, and mobility at 70
  • Erase the leading '1' and six financial zeros collapse to zero

Source: Sahil Bloom, The 5 Types of Wealth (2025)

Why Deferred Living Carries Compound Interest

Here is where smart, hard-working people get ambushed. Between ages twenty-five and fifty, millions play what psychologists call the Deferred Life Strategy: aggressively liquidating Time, Social, Mental, and Physical wealth to stockpile Financial wealth, assuming they can simply repurchase the first four at sixty. Unfortunately, compound interest works in reverse on neglect. Neglected friendships quietly dissolve, missed childhood summers never replay, and twenty years of chronic stress and five-hour sleep nights carry biological interest rates money can rarely reverse.

From Endless Status Game to Sufficiency Engine

Financial Wealth works best as a freedom engine, not an endless status contest. Kurt Vonnegut once told Joseph Heller, at a party thrown by a billionaire, that their host had made more money in a single day than Catch-22 had earned in its entire history. Heller replied that he had something the billionaire could never have: enough. Without a written definition of enough, every income jump simply pushes the goalpost further away.

Source: Kurt Vonnegut, 'Joe Heller', The New Yorker (2005)

The 10-Minute Sunday Life Portfolio Audit

Put this framework into practice this Sunday with a ten-minute Life Portfolio Audit. First, score all five buckets, Time, Social, Mental, Physical, and Financial, honestly from one to ten. Second, write one strict Anti-Goal for each pillar: non-negotiable boundaries you refuse to cross during busy seasons, such as never missing three family dinners in a row or never trading a morning walk for low-priority email. Finally, put one concrete thirty-minute deposit on Monday's calendar for whichever pillar scored lowest, so your life rebalances long before crisis forces it.
