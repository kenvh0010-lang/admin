Why Investors Stop Checking Accounts When Markets Drop

In 2009, economists Niklas Karlsson, George Loewenstein, and Duane Seppi analyzed investor login patterns in the Journal of Risk and Uncertainty and proved the Ostrich Effect. Here is when ignoring financial data helps and when it quietly hurts.

Why Portfolio Logins Vanish During Red Weeks

Notice how eagerly people check their brokerage app during a bull run, then stop opening the app the week markets drop?

  • Green market weeks trigger constant account refreshes
  • Red weeks make people avoid opening financial apps

Source: Karlsson, Loewenstein & Seppi, Journal of Risk and Uncertainty (2009)

Karlsson, Loewenstein & Seppi's Login Study

In 2009, economists Niklas Karlsson, George Loewenstein, and Duane Seppi analyzed Vanguard and Scandinavian pension logins in the Journal of Risk and Uncertainty. Account checks surged on up days and fell sharply during downturns.

  • Investors monitored portfolios heavily after market gains
  • Logins dropped sharply whenever indexes fell or stalled

Source: Karlsson, Loewenstein & Seppi, 'The Ostrich Effect', JRU (2009)

Healthy Market Calm vs Costly Bill Avoidance

They called this selective attention the Ostrich Effect. Ignoring daily stock noise helps long-term holders, yet avoiding credit statements, tax bills, and cash leaks lets penalties quietly multiply.

Automate Long-Term Assets, Audit Weekly Cash

Separate volatile investments from monthly cashflow: automate index contributions so market dips need zero monitoring, while auditing bills on a fixed weekly calendar.

The 15-Minute Friday Financial Check-In

Run a calm fifteen-minute Friday money check regardless of mood so bad news never compounds in the dark.

  • Review checking, upcoming bills, and subscriptions every Friday
  • Facts on a spreadsheet shrink anxiety faster than avoidance
